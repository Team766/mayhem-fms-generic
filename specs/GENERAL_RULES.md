# General rules

Rules that carry over from one FRC season to the next: conduct, safety, drive team behaviour and robot-to-robot
interaction. A new game spec starts its rules list here. The list was drawn from several recent FRC seasons; check
it again whenever the base moves to a new FRC season.

## How to tell a general rule from a season rule

- It's about conduct, safety, drive team behavior, or robot-to-robot interaction — not a specific field element or game piece.
- The same idea recurs across most of the seasons checked, even though the exact wording, thresholds, and named zones change.
- FRC renumbers its rules every year, so match rules by meaning and intent, not by rule number.
- If a rule only makes sense once you name that season's field element, game piece, or mechanism (a goal, a climb structure, a loading port, a piece-count limit), it's season-specific, not general.
- A rule can be "mostly general" with a season-specific detail (a threshold, an exception list) — those go in the borderline section with a suggested generic wording.

## General rules

### Conduct and fair play
- **No colluding for Ranking Points** (minor, flagged as Ranking-Point-affecting; current ref: G206) — a team or ALLIANCE may not collude with another to each purposefully violate a rule in order to influence Ranking Points.
- **No forcing an opponent into a violation** (major; G210) — a strategy not consistent with standard gameplay and clearly aimed at making the opponent ALLIANCE break a rule is not allowed.
- **No gaming a field element with a game piece** (major; G404) — a robot may not deliberately use a scoring element to make a field element's challenge easier or harder for anyone.
- **No collusive isolating of match play** (major; G419) — two or more robots that appear to a referee to be working together may not isolate or close off any major element of match play.

### Match flow
- **No delaying the start of a match** (major; G301) — a drive team member may not cause significant delays to the start of their match.

### Drive team
- **Stay behind the line during autonomous** (minor; G401) — each drive team member must remain in their staged area during auto; a member staged behind their starting line may not contact anything in front of it, except for safety, an E-Stop/A-Stop, or with referee permission.
- **No touching the robot or console during autonomous** (minor; G402) — a drive team member may not interact with the robot or its console during auto, except for personal/equipment safety or to press an E-Stop/A-Stop.
- **Only drivers/human players operate the robot** (major; G422) — the robot may be operated only by its own drivers and/or human players; a coach may only activate an E-Stop or A-Stop.
- **Stay in your designated area for the whole match** (minor; G421) — drivers, coaches, human players, and technicians must each remain within their assigned area for the entire match.
- **Coach may not touch game pieces** (minor; G426) — except for safety purposes.

### Robot interaction
- **No crossing into the opponent's side during autonomous** (major; G403) — a robot fully on the opponent's side of the field during auto may not contact an opponent robot.
- **No prolonged pinning** (minor, with an additional foul for every further interval it continues; G418) — a robot may not pin an opponent robot in place beyond a short time limit.
- **No deliberate attaching, tipping, or entangling** (major; G417) — a robot may not deliberately attach to, tip over, or entangle with an opponent robot.
- **No damaging or impairing an opponent robot** (major; G415/G416, in some seasons split into a contact-based rule and a general damage rule) — whether by deliberate action or by initiating contact inside the opponent's robot perimeter, directly or through a controlled game piece or an extended component.

### Robot limits
- **Stay within the extension limits** (major; G413) — a robot may not extend beyond the horizontal or vertical expansion limits set by the robot rules.
- **No damaging a game piece** (major; G406) — neither a robot nor a human player may damage a game piece.
- **No ejecting a game piece from the field** (major; G405) — except through a designated exit or scoring opening.
- **No grabbing, attaching to, or damaging arena elements** (major; G412) — apart from named, season-specific exceptions (e.g. the current climb mechanism).

## Borderline

- **Contact with an opponent's endgame structure** — the structure it names and the time window both change. Suggested wording: "A robot may not contact an opponent robot that is engaged with its endgame scoring structure during the match's final scoring window."
- **Simultaneous game-piece control limits** — the count and piece types are set by that year's game. Suggested wording: "A robot may not have greater-than-momentary control of more than the number of scoring elements this season's manual allows, directly or transitively."
- **Launching only from an own-side zone** — names that season's own-side scoring zone. Suggested wording: "A robot may not launch a scoring element toward its goal unless it is within its designated launch zone."
- **Bumper-zone/height compliance** — more an equipment check than a mid-match foul, and tied to that season's field furniture. Suggested wording: "A robot's bumpers must remain within the bumper zone at all times during the match."
- **Game-piece entry-point restrictions** — names that season's loading port. Suggested wording: "Scoring elements may only be introduced to the field by a human player or driver through the season's designated loading port(s)."
- **Reaching past a loading-station boundary** — the boundary is a season-specific field fixture. Suggested wording: "A drive team member may not extend any body part beyond the boundary of their loading/human-player station."

## Using and maintaining this list

Start a new game spec from this list, drop what doesn't apply that year, and add the game's own `MA<yy>` rules for its field elements and mechanisms. When the base moves to a new FRC season, re-check that season's rule list against the principles above and fold in anything that has become a recurring, non-season-specific idea.
